## 📋 Exercise Log

| Exercise                       | Sets | Reps   | Weight | Notes / Rationale                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ---- | ------ | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Primary Vertical Pull**   | 4    | 6-10   |        | Choose **Weighted Pull-Ups** _or_ a heavy **Lat Pulldown** variation. This is your main lat-width builder.                                       |
| **2. Primary Horizontal Pull** | 4    | 8-10   |        | Stick with **Barbell or Dumbbell Rows**. This is your main back-thickness builder. Focus on a hard squeeze.                                      |
| **3. Secondary Back Movement** | 3    | 10-12  |        | A **Chest-Supported Row** or **Cable Row** is perfect here. It provides a different angle and is less taxing on the lower back.                  |
| **4. Rear Delts & Traps**      | 3    | 12-15  |        | **Face Pulls**. Unbeatable for posture and hitting the upper back/rear delts. Keep the weight light and controlled.                              |
| **5. Primary Bicep Curl**      | 3-4  | 8-12   |        | A **Straight Barbell Curl** or **Dumbbell Curl** is perfect for hitting the main bicep peak.                                                     |
| **6. Brachialis Movement**     | 3    | 10-12  |        | **Dumbbell Hammer Curls**. This builds the brachialis muscle, adding thickness to your arms and improving your pulling strength.                 |
| **7. Finisher: Grip & Traps**  | 2-3  | 30-60s |        | **Farmer's Carry**. This single exercise provides intense grip, forearm, and trap stimulation, making isolated wrist curls unnecessary for most. |

### 🔹 Notes
- Perform **each movement with control**, focus on full range of motion.  
- Adjust intensity so you are **warm but not fatigued** at the end of the warmup.  
- Use this warmup before any push, pull, or legs session.  
- **Progressive Overload:** The goal is to get stronger in the given rep ranges over time. Once you can hit the top of the rep range for all sets, increase the weight.
- **Focus on Form:** With fewer sets, every single rep counts. Make them clean, controlled, and intentional.
- **Listen to Your Body:** If your forearms or biceps feel completely fatigued after the back movements, you can reduce the arm isolation work to 2 sets each. The back work should always be the priority on a pull day.
